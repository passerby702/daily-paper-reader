## 摘要
扩散模型越来越多地被用于分子结构和构象系综的建模，但其学习表示和分数的热力学意义仍然难以捉摸。为了消除这种模糊性，我们引入了一个在数学上一致的作用-算子框架，该框架与扩散模型原生兼容。通过将固定的分子环境定义为基作用$S_0(x)$，将炼金术扰动定义为算子$O(x)$，标准扩散加噪产生了有效的噪声作用和算子，其梯度和炼金术导数直接由模型的学习场表示。这种严格的自洽性使得一个“噪声算子桥”能够从端点系综和逐帧评估中读出自由能差（$\Delta F$）。在丙氨酸二肽系统的控制实验中，我们表明纳入物理归纳偏置能够部分恢复基作用和扰动算子。当应用于一个具有可忽略相空间重叠的具有挑战性的C6-H到C6-F配体-口袋非键扰动（185L/IND）时，我们的监督桥估算出的炼金术$\Delta F$与稳定的19态MBAR参考值相差约$1\ k_\mathrm{B}T$。最后，我们证明仅凭端点坐标和二元标签就足以部分恢复算子形状和中心自由能尺度，而无需任何力或作用监督。这项工作为将生成性分子扩散模型从黑箱坐标采样器转变为可审计的热力学估计器提供了一条严谨的路径。

## Abstract
Diffusion models are increasingly utilized for modeling molecular structures and conformational ensembles, yet the thermodynamic meaning of their learned representations and scores remains elusive. To resolve this ambiguity, we introduce a mathematically consistent action-operator framework natively compatible with diffusion models. By defining a fixed molecular environment as a base action $S_0(x)$ and an alchemical perturbation as an operator $O(x)$, standard diffusion noising induces effective noised actions and operators whose gradients and alchemical derivatives are directly represented by the model's learned fields. This rigorous self-consistency enables a ``noisy operator bridge'' capable of reading out free-energy differences ($ΔF$) from endpoint ensembles and per-frame evaluations. In controlled experiments on alanine dipeptide systems, we show that incorporating physical inductive biases enables partial recovery of the base action and perturbation operator. When applied to a challenging C6-H to C6-F ligand-pocket nonbonded perturbation (185L/IND) with negligible phase-space overlap, our supervised bridge estimates the alchemical $ΔF$ within approximately $1\ k_\mathrm{B}T$ of a stable 19-state MBAR reference. Finally, we demonstrate that endpoint coordinates and binary labels alone are sufficient to partially recover the operator shape and a centered free-energy scale without any force or action supervision. This work provides a rigorous path toward transforming generative molecular diffusion models from black-box coordinate samplers into auditable thermodynamic estimators.
