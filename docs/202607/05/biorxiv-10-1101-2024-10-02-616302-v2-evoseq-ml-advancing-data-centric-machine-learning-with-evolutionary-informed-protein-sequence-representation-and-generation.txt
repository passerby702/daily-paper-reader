# Abstract 21 From protein structure predicƟon to novel protein generaƟon, challenging protein engineering tasks have 22 been made possible by advancements in machine learning (ML). While largely driven by ML architecture 23 refinements, these advancements in ML-based protein engineering campaigns have leŌ the impact of data 24 curaƟon underexplored. In light of the growing wealth of labeled sequence data, data-centric advances (e.g. 25 prioriƟzing improvements in ML protein engineering tools through the curaƟon of high-quality, domain-26 specific training data) are increasingly preferred over model-centric advancements. ImplemenƟng datasets 27 that accurately reflect biological complexity and diversity has been shown to improve the efficiency of training 28 protein engineering ML tools. Here, we evaluate an ancestral sequence reconstrucƟon (ASR)-informed data 29 augmentaƟon strategy for training generaƟve and representaƟon-learning models in protein engineering. 30 Using ethylene-forming enzyme (EFE) as a model system, we show that variaƟonal autoencoder models 31 trained on ancestral and near-ancestral sequence datasets generate variants with improved predicted and 32 experimentally measured thermostability relaƟve to variants generated from modern-sequence training data. 33 All experimentally tested ancestral and ML-generated EFEs produced detectable ethylene, although ML-34 generated variants showed reduced acƟvity relaƟve to wild-type EFE, indicaƟng that the approach more 35 strongly captured stability-associated features than catalyƟc opƟmizaƟon. We further evaluated ASR-enriched 36 sequence sets for fine-tuning ESM2 representaƟons in endolysin and lysozyme C stability-classificaƟon tasks, 37 where ancestral representaƟons were compeƟƟve with modern-sequence fine-tuning in selected seƫngs. 38 Overall, this work supports ASR-informed data augmentaƟon as a promising strategy for stability-oriented 39 protein sequence generaƟon and moƟvates future work to couple ancestral sequence diversity with explicit 40 funcƟonal selecƟon. 41

# IntroducƟon 47 Recent advancements in machine learning (ML) highlight the criƟcal role of data quality and diversity in model 48 performance, shiŌing focus towards data-centric approaches that complement algorithmic innovaƟons 1,2 .49 This evolving perspecƟve underscores the importance of curaƟng diverse datasets in developing effecƟve ML 50 models. Unlike model-centric methods that are widely accessible through open-source plaƞorms, data-51 centric methods offer tailored insights to specific applicaƟons which enhances model learning capabiliƟes 52 beyond scoring metrics 3,4 . This trend is evident in the development of the GPT model, which evolved from 53 focusing on architectural improvements to prioriƟzing data quality and data collecƟon strategies 5. As a result, 54 focusing on data-centric approaches promises a profound contribuƟon toward ML-driven problem-solving in 55 disƟnct domains. These approaches have already exhibited potenƟal in fields ranging from finance to 56 healthcare, improving model reliability, scalability, trustworthiness, and generalizability 2,6–9 . This shiŌ towards 57 data-centric approaches is especially significant in areas demanding intricate analysis, like protein 58 engineering, where such strategies could enhance the understanding of the complex protein fitness 59 landscape 10,11 .60 In protein engineering, the adopƟon of data-centric approaches is paramount due to the field’s unique 61 challenges. The protein fitness landscape is complex and rugged, with minor alteraƟons to protein sequences 62 significantly impacƟng funcƟonality and stability 12–14 . Despite advances in applying ML that have facilitated 63 the discovery of high-fitness proteins 15,16 , these models struggle with imbalanced datasets and the scarcity of 64 data across protein families. This concern underscores the necessity of reevaluaƟng our strategies towards 65 curaƟng training data 10,11,17–20 . A focus on enhancing the diversity and quality of datasets is crucial, as it 66 ensures that ML models are trained on data that accurately reflect the intricate biological properƟes of 67 proteins. Establishing a method to provide more effecƟve training datasets for ML models will pave the way 68 for strategically advancing protein engineering campaigns. 69 Built on the foundaƟon of evoluƟonary biology and molecular phylogeneƟcs, ancestral sequence 70 reconstrucƟon (ASR) constructs phylogeneƟc trees from an alignment of extant sequences and subsequently 71 implements subsƟtuƟon models to infer ancestral sequences - sequences that are typically diverse, and 72 stable 21–24 . Therefore, we hypothesized that enriching mulƟple sequence alignments - typically consisƟng of 73 modern sequences - with high-quality ancestral sequences would be a powerful data-centric approach to 74 protein engineering. 75 Furthermore, we explored methods to exploit the inherent staƟsƟcal uncertainƟes of ASR to dramaƟcally 76 expand the number of ancestral sequences from which ML models can be trained. Guided by various 77 subsƟtuƟon models for mutaƟons, ASR assigns posterior probabiliƟes to amino acids at various posiƟons to 78 infer ancestral sequences at a given node in a tree. Typical ASR campaigns reconstruct ancestors by picking 79 amino acids with the highest probability. However, sampling from a broad distribuƟon of amino acids can 80 generate ancestral proteins that are funcƟonal and, in some cases, more efficient or offer novel funcƟonaliƟes 81 compared to the most probable ancestors 25,26 . Even when incorporaƟng alternaƟve amino acids with a 82 posterior probability as low as 30%, reconstructed proteins maintained their overall funcƟon 25 . Given its 83 robustness to generate diverse and funcƟonal libraries despite staƟsƟcal uncertainƟes, ASR can generate up 84 to a billion unique sequences for a protein with 15 posiƟons that each have four high-likelihood amino acids. 85 86 Therefore, with the potenƟal to generate largescale high-quality sequences, ASR represents a potenƟally 87 impacƞul data-centric protein engineering approach. Prior ASR studies have shown that alternaƟve ancestral 88 states arising from staƟsƟcal uncertainty can oŌen retain funcƟon, moƟvaƟng the use of ancestral ensembles 89

# Methods 130 

## GeneraƟve Model for Novel Protein Sequence GeneraƟon 131 We aimed to test whether incorporaƟng evoluƟonary data into the training of generaƟve models yields 132 protein sequences that are both novel and structurally stable. To this end, we focused on the strain PK2 EFE 133 and employed a VAE as our generaƟve ML model. Subsequent computaƟonal analyses (i.e., AlphaFold 32 ,134 FoldX 33 , UMAP visualizaƟon 35 ) were conducted on the sequences generated by this model to assess their 135 quality. 136 

Ancestral Sequence ReconstrucƟon 137 We extracted the evoluƟonary informaƟon of EFE using our AP-LASR 36 soŌware, a tool designed to reconstruct 138 ensembles of ancestral proteins by leveraging the phylogeneƟc tree of a query protein sequence 37 . This tool 139 has facilitated ASR by fully automaƟng the reconstrucƟon process from iniƟal BLAST search, mulƟple 140 sequence alignment by MAFFT 38 to tree phylogeny predicƟons via IQ-TREE2 39 .141

For this study, we focused on two AP-LASR outputs: maximum-probability ancestral sequences (ASR-Max), 142 and diversity-enhanced near-ancestral sequence ensembles (ASR-Dist). ASR-Dist was generated from the 143 sitewise marginal posterior probabiliƟes reported in the IQ-TREE ASR.state file. For each ancestral node, 144 amino acids with posterior probability above a defined threshold were retained as allowable states at that 145 posiƟon, and sequences were then sampled from these allowable states to generate near-ancestral libraries. 146 Therefore, ASR-Dist should be interpreted as a diversity-enhanced sampling of plausible near-ancestral 147 sequence space based on marginal posterior uncertainty, rather than as exact draws from the full joint 148 posterior distribuƟon of ancestral sequences. Unless otherwise noted, we used a posterior-probability 149 threshold of 0.2 based on prior evidence that ancestral proteins can be robust to moderate posterior 150 uncertainty and because this threshold provided a pracƟcal compromise between library diversity and 151 retenƟon of high-probability ancestral states 25,40 .152 To evaluate how the choice of probabilisƟc sequence reconstrucƟon methods influences generaƟve model 153 performance, we compared IQ-TREE2’s maximum likelihood approach against BAli-Phy’s Bayesian inference 154 method. BAli-Phy uses a Bayesian inference method to simultaneously esƟmate sequence alignment, tree 155 phylogeny, and model parameters, providing full posterior distribuƟons of ancestral sequences 41 . Because 156 BAli-Phy jointly esƟmates alignment, tree topology, model parameters, and ancestral states, it can account 157 for addiƟonal sources of uncertainty relaƟve to workflows that condiƟon on a fixed alignment and tree. This 158 increased uncertainty modeling comes at substanƟally greater computaƟonal cost. 159 In brief, the same mulƟple sequence alignment generated by AP-LASR for IQ-TREE was used to iniƟalize eight 160 independent Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) runs in BAli-Phy. The opƟmal amino acid subsƟtuƟon model 161 for the runs, lg08 42 , was determined using IQ-TREE’s ModelFinder 43 . AŌer running simulaƟons for seven days, 162 three runs were selected for convergence by minimizing the average standard deviaƟon of split frequencies 163 (ASDSF) and the maximum of the standard deviaƟon of split frequencies (MSDSF). For convergence analysis, 164 the first 2,000 iteraƟons of each run were discarded to account for the bias of the iniƟal random starƟng guess 165 (i.e., burn-in). The ensemble of ancestors at each ancestral node were subsequently retrieved from the BAli-166 Phy runs, using the MAP tree topology to define ancestor nodes. ETE4 Toolkit 44 was used to define all ancestor 167 nodes on the MAP tree based on each node’s set of children sequences, and Dendropy 45 was used to sample 168 ancestral sequences at each ancestral node for all BAli-Phy output trees. For more targeted protein 169 generaƟon, we sampled the sequences from four nodes represenƟng high-stability ancestors obtained from 170 various evoluƟonary Ɵmescales (Node10, Node13, Node253, and Node384, as shown in Figure S1) 37 .171 

Automated BAli-Phy replicate workflow and ASR generaƟon 172 To improve robustness of alignment, topology, and ancestral sequence esƟmates, we implemented an 173 automated BAli-Phy–based workflow that runs mulƟple independent MCMC replicates and summarizes a 174 consistent subset for downstream ASR. The pipeline (executed on an HPC cluster via SLURM with BAli-Phy 175 packaged in a Singularity container) launches five BAli-Phy replicates from the same input FASTA, extracts 176 posterior values from each run aŌer burn-in, and selects the three runs with the most similar post–burn-in 177 median posterior values to exclude obvious outlier chains prior to downstream summarizaƟon. These 178 selected replicates are then passed to bp-analyze to generate a consensus summary and the maximum a 179 posteriori (MAP) tree (MAP.tree). A consensus alignment is reordered to match the MAP tree Ɵp order 180 (alignment-cat) and summarize-ancestors is used to generate an ASR.state file and associated outputs for 181 downstream AP-LASR analyses. Detailed, step-by-step usage instrucƟons (inputs/outputs, commands, and 182 troubleshooƟng) are provided in the Supplementary InstrucƟons and in the accompanying repository. All BAli-183 Phy tools are executed within the container, and intermediate outputs (posterior summaries, consensus 184 alignments, and trees) are retained to facilitate troubleshooƟng and rerunning summary steps without 185 repeaƟng the full MCMC. 186

GeneraƟve Model Architecture and Training 199 VAE was selected for its proficiency in generaƟng new data points that are coherent with the training data 47,48 .200 We employed one-hot encoding to transform the sequences into a format suitable for computaƟonal 201 processing. Feature extracƟon from these one-hot encoded sequences was performed using a 1D 202 ConvoluƟonal Neural Network (CNN) layer, allowing us to capture the local sequence paƩerns effecƟvely. The 203 architecture of our VAE was designed with a latent space dimensionality of 100, ensuring sufficient complexity 204 to capture the nuances of protein sequence variability. AddiƟonally, we incorporated batch normalizaƟon 205 within the network to facilitate smoother and more stable learning dynamics. This combinaƟon of 1D CNN 206 for feature learning and batch normalizaƟon for opƟmizaƟon contributed to refining the model’s ability to 207 generate meaningful protein sequences. Novel sequences were generated by sampling from the learned 208 latent representaƟons aŌer loss minimizaƟon in the validaƟon set. 209

## EFE Protein Analysis 280 

Plasmid ConstrucƟon 281 DNA encoding wild-type EFE from strain PK2 was constructed as previously described 59 . Ten EFEs generated 282 by the simple VAE trained on diverse ancestors were chosen randomly for experimental characterizaƟon. DNA 283 sequences encoding ancestral and ML-generated EFEs were cloned into the same modified pET28a + vector by 284 IDTdna, with each gene fragment codon opƟmized for expression in E. coli BL21. The modified pET28a + vector 285 includes a sequence encoding an N-terminal His 6 fused to EFE sequences with a tobacco etch virus (TEV) 286 protease cleavage site, a kanamycin resistance gene, and a T7 promoter regulated by a lac operator sequence 287 to facilitate isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG)-induced transcripƟon. Plasmids were transformed 288 into E. coli BL21 (DE3) cells (ThermoFisher ScienƟfic, EC0114) following the manufacturer’s instrucƟons. 289 Glycerol stocks were created from single colonies selected for kanamycin resistance (50 mg/mL) on Luria broth 290 (LB) agar plates. 291 

Protein ProducƟon and PurificaƟon 292 Protein producƟon and purificaƟon followed previously published protocols 59 . Briefly, 5-mL LB liquid cultures, 293 supplemented with 50 mg/mL kanamycin, were inoculated from glycerol stocks and grown overnight in a 294 shaking incubator set at 37 °C and 250 rpm. Cultures were diluted into 50 mL terrific broth to an OD 600 of 295 0.01-0.1 and shaking was conƟnued at 37 °C and 250 rpm. Once the cells reached an OD 600 of 0.8-1, they were 296 induced with 0.2 mM IPTG overnight. Cells were pelleted by centrifugaƟon at 6,130 x g for 10 min at 4 °C 297 before sonicaƟng them in buffer A (50 mM NaH 2PO 4, pH 8.0, containing 500 mM NaCl and 10 mM imidazole) 298 using a Fisherbrand TM 505 sonicator. # DISCUSSION 513  

> In this work, we explored the integraƟon of evoluƟonary informaƟon into ML models for protein engineering.

Specifically, we 514 evaluated whether ASR-derived sequence ensembles can serve as data augmentaƟon sets for ML-guided 515 protein engineering. In the EFE generaƟve-modeling workflow, computaƟonal and wet-lab studies showed 516 that ancestral and near-ancestral training datasets shiŌed VAE-generated sequences toward improved 517 thermostability compared to VAE-generated sequences trained on modern sequences alone. However, the 518 ML-generated EFEs exhibited lower ethylene-forming acƟvity than wild-type EFE, indicaƟng that the model 519 captured stability-associated sequence features more effecƟvely than catalyƟc opƟmizaƟon. In the ESM2 fine-520 tuning workflow, ASR-derived representaƟons were compeƟƟve with modern-sequence fine-tuning for 521 stability classificaƟon, with modest advantages in selected classifier/family combinaƟons but no universal 522 improvement across all models tested. 523 Our approach combined ASR with generaƟve modeling and language model fine-tuning to enhance both 524 sequence generaƟon and fitness predicƟon. The results demonstrated that sequences generated from 525 evoluƟonary-informed datasets exhibited improved characterisƟcs, parƟcularly in thermal stability, compared 526 to those derived from models trained solely on modern sequences. This highlights the potenƟal of ASR to 527 enrich training data for generaƟve models, leading to more diverse and higher-quality outputs. 528 Briefly comparing maximum likelihood and Bayesian inference approaches to infer ancestral training 529 sequences, IQ-TREE and BAli-Phy workflows produced ancestral datasets with similar downstream stability 530 and diversity profiles in this EFE case study. This finding should not be interpreted as demonstraƟng equivalent 531 ancestral inference quality. Rather, it suggests that for the downstream ML objecƟves examined here, IQ-532 TREE-derived marginal posterior sampling was sufficient to generate sequence sets with similar predicted 533 stability and diversity properƟes, while being substanƟally more computaƟonally tractable. 534 A key factor potenƟally contribuƟng to this similarity is the sampling strategy. By incorporaƟng sequences 535 from all nodes in the phylogeneƟc tree, both methods likely capture a wide range of ancestral states, including 536 those with varying degrees of certainty. This comprehensive sampling may balance out the theoreƟcal 537 advantages of each approach, resulƟng in similar overall performance. The stability results indicate that both 538 methods generate sequences with comparable thermodynamic properƟes, suggesƟng IQ-Tree’s point 539 esƟmates are as robust as BAli-Phy’s distribuƟon-based esƟmates for maintaining protein stability. The 540 equivalent sequence variability and semanƟc diversity imply that IQ-Tree’s maximum likelihood approach 541 explores sequence space as effecƟvely as BAli-Phy’s Bayesian method. 542 As demonstrated in both computaƟonal predicƟons and wet-lab characterizaƟon, the ancestral training 543 sequences and evoluƟonary-informed generated sequences exhibit enhanced thermostability. The precise 544 mechanisms underlying this shiŌ remain unclear, it is possible that the generaƟve model, when applied to 545 modern sequences, miƟgates noise from low-quality sequences (in terms of epistasis and folding). Conversely, 546 the ancestral training data may inherently encompass stabilizing interacƟons. Consequently, the generated 547 sequences from ancestral proteins consistently demonstrate superior stability compared to those derived 548 from modern sequences. 549 These findings highlight the potenƟal benefits of incorporaƟng evoluƟonary data in generaƟve models for 550 protein design. Ancestral sequences appear to combine desirable properƟes such as structural stability with 551 an expanded exploraƟon of sequence space. This combinaƟon could prove valuable in generaƟng novel 552 protein variants that maintain crucial ancestral characterisƟcs while introducing funcƟonal innovaƟons. By 553 leveraging the broader semanƟc diversity of ancestral sequences, generaƟve models could potenƟally access 554
